# Dry storage and rehydration of the functionalized GFET chip

**Date:** 2026-11-12
**Status:** literature check — no measurements on our chips.
**Question:** is the rinse-with-water → dry → vacuum-desiccator pattern the standard way to store a PBASE/DNA graphene chip between steps that are days apart, and does rehydration give back what drying took?

Companion note: [fluorescence quenching and drying](2026-11-12-fluorescence-quenching-and-drying.md) covers *why* drying pins the DNA layer. This note covers *what other people do*, how much of it the composite tolerates, and how to organise a multi-day workflow.

---

## Short answer

**Drying is legitimate and is the manufacturer's own storage recommendation — but the fragile part is not the graphene.** It is the non-covalent pyrene/DNA interface, and that interface loses something (`~30%` of attached DNA in the one quantitative QCM study, plus a dried-in flattening) that plain rehydration does not return. The chip will still look perfect by AFM height and by Dirac point afterwards.

The useful reframe: **water does more good than harm to this composite, and desiccation is a logistics tool, not a preservation one.** For multi-day gaps, a wet chip at 4 °C is both easier and less damaging than a dry chip in a desiccator. Dry it when an instrument demands dry — AFM — and put that step last.

## What the manufacturer actually specifies

Graphenea's measurement protocol for the S20/S21 (the chips we use), verbatim:

> "storage of the chips in a low humidity environment (N2 cabinet, desiccator, or vacuum) is highly recommended"

> "Ideally store in inert atmosphere or under vacuum in order to minimize adsorption of unknown species from the ambient air"

> "Graphene on SiO2 is often p-doped after exposure to air due to the adsorption of water molecules and other adsorbates with the effect that the Dirac point is shifted to positive gate voltages … In addition, a large hysteresis is observed between the forward and backward sweeps of a transfer curve. Immersing the GFET chip in acetone for at least 12h reduces doping."

> "This treatment is specially recommended for measurements in dry conditions, and is not necessary for liquid gating experiments"

Plus: no ultrasonication, no plasma treatment, no strongly oxidizing reagents, no metal tweezers against the device area, ESD precautions.

Three things follow, and they are easy to misread:

1. **The desiccator recommendation is ours to follow** — it is not folklore, and it is aimed at exactly the problem we would otherwise have (adsorbed species, drifting Dirac point, hysteresis).
2. **It is about the electrical baseline, not about a DNA brush.** The document says nothing about preserving surface chemistry, because it is not written for functionalized chips.
3. **The acetone/IPA doping-reduction recipe applies to bare chips only.** On a PBASE/DNA chip it would strip the whole functionalization. Do not use it.

## Is the electrical state recoverable?

Mostly yes, and slowly. Adsorbed water on graphene is a well-characterised source of hysteresis and Dirac-point drift, and the direction of movement with humidity is understood — so a wet chip's electrical state is a moving target that has to be allowed to settle rather than measured immediately after rehydration. Reusable aptamer GFETs are regenerated by an annealing step and their transfer curve returns to the starting state, which says the *electrical* baseline is recoverable by wetting and heating.

Wet stability is also not the problem people expect: microfluidic GFETs held in 1× PBS showed only a slight change in charge-neutrality voltage after **5 h** of continuous incubation. Days in buffer are a storage question, not a stability one.

## What dry storage is genuinely good for

Worth stating, because the case for drying is real:

- **Contamination.** A dry, foil-wrapped chip in a desiccator does not accumulate whatever is in the air or in the fridge.
- **Biology.** 100 mM ethanolamine at pH 9 (our block) is a growth medium; PBS at 37 °C is not much better. A dry chip cannot grow anything.
- **Handling.** A dry chip can be moved, shipped, imaged elsewhere, and left on a bench for an hour without dying.
- **Electrical drift.** Less adsorbate, smaller hysteresis.

The cost is confined to one layer, but that layer is the one the experiment depends on.

## Layer by layer: what survives, with numbers

| Layer | Binding / behaviour | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Pyrene → graphene (PBASE)** | Strongly adsorbed, effectively permanently: adsorption energy **−1.63 eV** (chains lying down) or **−1.28 eV** (chains standing), of which the pyrene contributes **−0.88 eV**. Barrier between the two conformations only **0.20 eV**, so the chain is mobile at room temperature. | DFT + 3D-RISM, Oishi et al. 2022 |
| **Linker conformation** | **Dry favours "lying down"; water favours "standing up."** Hydration lowers the energy of the standing conformation relative to lying down (ΔE 0.35 → 0.28 eV), and the authors conclude the standing conformation "appears more frequently in a solution in comparison to that under vacuum conditions." | Oishi et al. 2022 |
| **DNA on the linker** | **~30% of the DNA that adsorbs is lost to running-buffer rinsing** (weakly attached fraction); of what remains, **up to 26% is nonspecific**; ethanolamine blocking of unreacted NHS measured at **~80% efficiency**. PBASE gives the highest probe density of the three linkers compared (pyrene > acridine > fluorenyl). | QCM-D, Domingues et al. 2025 |
| **PBASE layer integrity** | Long PBASE soaks over-build: 4 h at 50 mM in DMF was optimal, and longer incubations aggregate pyrene into a bilayer-like structure. Aggregation is not reversed by rinsing. | reviewed in Mishyn et al. 2022 |
| **DNA conformation (dry)** | Graphene surfaces are not passive towards adsorbed DNA: layered graphene–mica **melts DNA origami on contact**. On HOPG, ambient humidity changes the measured height of DNA, i.e. hydration state changes conformation — and dry AFM heights are therefore a function of RH. | Pham et al. 2018; Zhou et al. 2007 |
| **Duplex** | Stable dry. The risk is not drying but the low-ionic-strength rinse used before drying. | see companion note |
| **Graphene** | The robust layer. Degrades by doping and hysteresis, not by drying, provided no plasma, sonication or oxidiser. | Graphenea protocol |

## What rehydration gives back

**Recovers well:**

- The **linker conformation**. The standing-up transition has a 0.20 eV barrier — about 8 kT at room temperature — so it is thermally accessible and does not need to be forced. This is the one part of the composite that genuinely does recover from drying, and it is *promoted* by water rather than merely permitted by it.
- Hydration of the DNA, B-form geometry, secondary structure, FAM brightness (in buffer, pH 7.4).
- The electrical baseline, over minutes to tens of minutes, with drift.

**Does not recover:**

- The **base–surface pinning** created by drying. Rehydration restores the solvent; nothing in it lifts a base that is π-stacked on an aromatic surface (adhesion of 3–15 kT per base, tens to hundreds of kT for a contact patch). See [the companion note](2026-11-12-fluorescence-quenching-and-drying.md).
- **Pyrene aggregation**, if it has happened.
- Anything **adsorbed from air** while it sat.
- The **~30% weakly attached DNA** — though that fraction went during the rinse, not the drying.

## The uncomfortable consequence

**Each dry/wet cycle degrades exactly the property the experiment measures** — hybridization competence and upright duplex geometry — while leaving intact the two properties you are most likely to check: AFM height (a flattened layer still has height) and Dirac point (the electrical baseline recovers). The damage is invisible to both of those readouts and visible only in the FAM signal that the cycles exist to obtain.

So the cycle count becomes a covariate in every result. Record it per chip.

## Recommended practice for a multi-day workflow

1. **Multi-day gaps: store wet at 4 °C**, foil-wrapped, in 1× PBS with 0.02% sodium azide. No dry cycle, no pinning, no doping churn. A fridge is cheaper and less damaging than a desiccator for preservation.
2. **Dry only when an instrument demands it, and put that step last.** One cycle per experiment: hybridize wet → 3 × PBS → image fluorescence wet in PBS → water (cold and fast) or 0.15–0.3 M ammonium acetate → N₂ dry → desiccator → dry AFM.
3. **Never dry between attachment and hybridization.** That is the one interval where a dry step costs the most, because the pinned 3′ end is where the complement has to nucleate.
4. **Count and record cycles** — 0, 1, 2 — in the chip's line in the run record.
5. If dry storage is to become the default handling mode, the literature-backed upgrade is a **protectant in the final rinse**: 5–10% trehalose or sucrose before drying, the standard way to preserve dried biomolecules, and the role the commercial desiccated-graphene products fill with a proprietary additive.
6. **Rehydrate through humidity, then buffer** — sealed box over water 30–60 min, then PBS 10–30 min — rather than plunging a dry chip into liquid.

## The experiment that would settle it for our composite

No published study measures **hybridization efficiency as a function of dry/wet cycles on PBASE-graphene** — the gap is real, and we are unusually well set up to close it.

| Element | Design |
| --- | --- |
| Samples | 12 chips from one attachment batch: 4 arms × 3 chips |
| Arms | **0, 1, 2, 3 dry/wet cycles** (cycle = cold water rinse → N₂ dry → desiccator ≥ 24 h → humid-chamber rehydration → PBS) |
| Then | identical hybridization: 1 µM complement, 30 min at 37 °C, 3 × PBS, 45 °C/5 min stringency, 2 × PBS |
| Readout 1 | blue-over-white ratio on the same fields, fixed manual camera settings, wet in PBS |
| Readout 2 | dry AFM height and Rq on the same chips afterwards (tap mode) — the cycle where morphology and signal diverge is the informative one |
| Control arms | PBASE + block only (no template) at each cycle count, to separate adsorption from hybridization |
| Prediction | signal falls monotonically with cycle count while AFM height barely moves. If AFM height and signal fall together, the loss is material (DNA or PBASE leaving); if only the signal falls, it is orientation and accessibility. |

That last distinction is the useful one: it says whether to fix the rinse (material loss) or the drying/annealing schedule (orientation).

| PBASE does not desorb under normal handling | high | DFT adsorption energy −1.28 to −1.63 eV |
